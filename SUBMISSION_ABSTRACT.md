# ECHOES — Project Abstract

Since the beginning of lunar and planetary exploration, spacecraft have left behind landers, rovers, instruments, and other hardware. These machines may have completed their missions or stopped communicating, but the places they explored and the science they helped produce are still worth understanding. For students, that history is often scattered across photographs, mission pages, maps, and technical records.

ECHOES brings part of that story into one interactive, browser-based 3D experience. Visitors can move through a navigable model of the solar system, explore selected lunar and Martian environments, inspect spacecraft representations, and read mission records covering objectives, instruments, milestones, discoveries, and mission status. The planetary environments use terrain informed by MOLA and LOLA elevation data. The project also draws on JPL orbital elements, NASA mission records, spacecraft models, and credited archival media.

The project currently includes ten registered mission vehicles or spacecraft representations: Spirit, Opportunity, Sojourner, InSight, Apollo 12, Apollo 14, Apollo 15, Apollo 16, Apollo 17, and Voyager 1. Opportunity (MER-B) is the most developed journey: it includes a chapter-based mission story, a spatial waypoint route, and an embedded 3D documentary. The other mission journeys are chapter-based, and archival media coverage is not yet complete across all of them.

ECHOES is designed to help school-age space enthusiasts connect a machine to the place it explored and the knowledge it helped create. Rather than presenting every mission as equally complete, the experience makes its current scope and limitations visible.
