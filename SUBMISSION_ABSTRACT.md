# ECHOES — Project Abstract

NASA's missions to the Moon and Mars left behind landers, rovers, instruments, and other hardware that helped make exploration possible. Although these machines are no longer all operating, the places where they worked and the science they enabled remain important. Their stories are spread across mission archives, photographs, maps, and technical records, making it difficult for school-age space enthusiasts to understand what each machine did, where it went, and why it mattered.

ECHOES is an interactive, browser-based 3D exploration experience that brings these machines and their mission stories into the worlds they explored. Visitors navigate a model of the solar system, explore selected lunar and Martian environments, inspect spacecraft representations, and discover structured mission records covering objectives, instruments, milestones, discoveries, and mission status. The experience uses planetary terrain informed by MOLA and LOLA elevation data, JPL orbital elements, spacecraft models and reconstructions, and credited mission imagery, with source references provided alongside mission information.

ECHOES includes ten registered mission vehicles or spacecraft representations, including Apollo lunar hardware and Mars exploration vehicles. The Opportunity (MER-B) experience is the most developed example: visitors can explore its chapter-based mission story, follow a spatial waypoint route, and view an embedded 3D documentary. Other missions are currently presented through chapter-based records rather than equivalent spatial journeys, and archival media coverage is not yet complete across all missions.

By connecting the hardware, its planetary setting, and the science it made possible in one explorable experience, ECHOES aims to help school-age audiences understand and remember the machines humanity left behind on the Moon and Mars.
